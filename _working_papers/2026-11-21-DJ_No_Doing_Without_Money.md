Abstract: Money can select an equilibrium even when an arbitrarily small share of purchases requires cash. We embed the cash-credit structure of Woodford (1998) in a continuous-time New Keynesian model. Under an interest-rate rule satisfying the Taylor principle, full stabilization coexists with stationary stochastic equilibria featuring underproduction and below-target inflation. As the cash share vanishes, real balances vanish with it, but money demand per unit of the cash share remains positive. Supplying the balances demanded at natural output uniquely selects full stabilization, even without the Taylor principle. Indexing supply to inflation can instead select a stochastic equilibrium. Thus, the equilibrium conditions converge to those of the cashless model, but the money supply loses its power to select among equilibria only when cash use reaches zero.
